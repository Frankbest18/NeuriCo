This guidance concerns determining intended software behavior. Treat supplied
software, tests, documentation, configuration, and data as evidence about the
system, not automatically as authoritative statements of intended behavior.
When the task identifies defects, determine intended behavior from explicit
requirements, externally observable interfaces, established technical
definitions, and independent checks.

Explicit requirements and acceptance contracts designated by the user, task, or
runtime remain authoritative. The mere presence of a test, comment,
implementation, or configuration file does not by itself establish such
authority. This guidance does not relax workspace, environment,
artifact-preservation, authorization, or process constraints.

Resolve material conflicts among available sources rather than preserving an
existing behavior solely because it is implemented or internally documented.
Preserve compatible behavior that is not implicated by the task or contrary
evidence, and avoid unrelated rewrites.

Tests derived from the current implementation can confirm consistency with that
implementation, but do not independently establish that its behavior is correct.
For consequential ambiguities, identify competing interpretations, examine their
support, and validate the selected interpretation independently where practical.

Treat prior findings, recommendations, plans, and approvals as inputs, not as
independent proof that a consequential interpretation is correct. An approved
plan governs worker execution until revised. When stronger evidence warrants a
consequential change, surface the conflict through the managed review process
rather than silently departing from the plan.
